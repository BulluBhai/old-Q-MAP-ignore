EXECUTIVE INTERPRETATION (plain English; every number below is from this run's calculated results)

1. Question investigated
  For this instrument, which moving averages (type and period) has price historically interacted with most
  reliably - coming within the proximity zone and then moving in the expected direction over the following
  bars - and do the leaders chosen on the earlier (in-sample) part of the history keep that behaviour on the
  later (out-of-sample) part? And is the best result stronger than the best result that chance alone produces
  on the same prices? Instrument: NSE:BETADEMO Daily (dataset 2).

2. Data and period
  1,500 bars from 01-Jan-19 to 30-Sep-24 (bar size: Daily (Q-MAP registered dataset; not re-aggregated)). The
  ranking used only the first 1050 bars (01-Jan-19 to 09-Jan-23, in-sample); the later 450 bars (10-Jan-23 to
  30-Sep-24, out-of-sample) were kept aside to check the ranking afterwards.

3. What the analysis found
  Of 482 moving averages tested (480 with enough interactions to be ranked), the highest in-sample score was
  EMA-37 with an affinity score of 66.13 out of 100: price came near it 44 time(s) in-sample and moved the
  expected way 5 bars later in 63.64% of 44 usable outcome(s). The other Top-5 findings: EMA-248, SMA-38,
  SMA-49, EMA-21.

4. What the scores and percentages mean
  The affinity score (0-100) combines four parts with fixed weights: how often price interacted with the MA,
  how often it then moved the expected way, how large that move was relative to its variability, and how
  steady the success rate was over time. It is a ranking score, not a probability or a forecast. 'In-sample
  success %' is the share of qualifying in-sample outcomes in which price moved the expected way after 5 bars:
  higher after touching the MA from above (support), lower after touching it from below (resistance).

5. Comparison with chance
  Verdict: NOT DISTINGUISHABLE FROM CHANCE. On 200 block-shuffled copies of the same in-sample prices (where
  any real link with a moving average is destroyed) the best score averaged 72.59 and reached 87.36 at most;
  the real best score was 66.13 (chance p-value 0.8657). Luck alone often produced a best score this high, so
  the ranking may be chance and should be treated as unproven. With 200 shuffles the smallest possible chance
  p-value is 0.0050, so p-values are approximate at that resolution.

6. Comparison with the market's own movement (drift baseline)
  Over the same in-sample bars, price was higher or lower 5 bars later anyway in 51.36% of cases (weighted to
  EMA-37's own support and resistance outcomes). Its 63.64% success is therefore 12.27 percentage points above
  that baseline. Out-of-sample its success rate was 68.00% over 25 outcome(s), against a baseline of 54.50%
  (edge 13.50 points). The baseline is reported only; the score still compares with 50%, as in V1.

7. Main limitations
  The chance test uses one price history and one shuffle model (blocks of 15 bars), so it does not prove that
  a result will persist; the out-of-sample check rests on 25 outcome(s); the chance threshold 0.05, the 15-bar
  blocks, the strength rule, Group % and the ATR option are provisional beta defaults, not owner-approved
  methodology; Directed IR and the score weights are pending owner review. The full list is under 'How to read
  this' in the findings file and in the reports.

8. What to investigate next
  Do not rely on this ranking; try a longer history, another timeframe or other instruments; look at the
  detailed tables (Excel Top_N and Rankings sheets, CSV files) for the evidence behind each number.

How to read this
  - A historical rank is a description of the past, not evidence that a moving average will work in future.
  - The chance test compares the real #1 score with the #1 scores found on block-shuffled copies of the same in-sample prices. It allows for the many candidates tested, but it is still a test on one history: a result that stands out from chance on one instrument should be confirmed on others before it is relied on.
  - The chance result is conditional on the shuffle model: blocks of bars keep each bar's shape, the overall move and short-term patterns within a block, but not longer trends or volatility regimes. Standing out from this simulated chance is not proof of a profitable or persistent trading edge.
  - With N shuffles the smallest possible chance p-value is 1/(N+1) (0.0050 with 200), so chance p-values are approximate at that resolution; use more shuffles (for example 1000) for a finer value.
  - The chance threshold (0.05), the 15-bar blocks, the relative strength rule (+/-20% median), Group % (10), the ATR option (14 bars, 1.0 x) and MA warm-up are PROVISIONAL beta defaults, documented and configurable; they are not owner-approved final methodology.
  - The binomial p-values in the tables are NOT corrected for the many candidates tested; they are kept for reference only. Use the chance p-value to judge a finding.
  - Success is scored against 50% (as in V1). The drift baseline (what price did anyway over the same bars) is reported alongside; a success rate close to its baseline mostly reflects the stock's own trend.
  - Neighbouring MA periods are almost identical. V2 groups them (Group %), so each Top-N place is a distinct finding; MA types are not grouped with each other, so an EMA and an SMA of similar period can both appear.
  - Forward windows longer than the primary window overlap each other, so those statistics are not independent observations.
  - Forward Return (Directed IR) methodology is PENDING OWNER REVIEW. The V1 formula is kept unchanged.
  - The official affinity weight scheme is still undecided. The four V1 weights are kept in their original proportions and normalised.
  - Out-of-sample samples can be small. Where the OOS sample is below the minimum, consistency is reported as insufficient evidence.
  - These results describe historical data only. They do not predict or guarantee future price behaviour or future performance of any reference. This is a research tool, not a trading signal or recommendation.
