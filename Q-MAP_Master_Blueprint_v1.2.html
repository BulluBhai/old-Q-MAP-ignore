Master Project Intent, Requirements & Architecture Blueprint

Document version: 1.2
Status: Proposed revised product and architecture baseline — pending Project Owner review and approval
Prepared: 1 November 2026
Document purpose: Explain what Q-MAP is intended to do, how its parts fit together, how it should be used, and the principles that should guide future development.

In plain English: Q-MAP is one integrated quantitative research platform. Version 1.2 change summary

Version 1.2 preserves the substantive intent of v1.1 and incorporates the architecture and application-design decisions discussed after that revision. It is a proposed revision for Project Owner review; it does not itself authorise code changes or claim that the design is already implemented.

Key changes and clarifications: - Q-MAP is organised at the top level around two major modules: the Database Module and the Analytical Module. - The Database Module is a prerequisite for analysis: the Analytical Workbench must verify that the data required for a requested analysis is registered, accessible, sufficiently complete and suitable. The dependency is on the readiness of the specific required data, not merely on whether a database exists. - Q-MAP will have two separate user interfaces: Database Manager and Analytical Workbench. - The target application layout is one shared Q-MAP_APP folder with two main Python entry points—database_manager.py and analytical_workbench.py—and shared plus specialised supporting components. The exact internal folder layout is subject to an audit of the current code before implementation. - The two GUIs are separate entry points into one integrated platform. They share the Data Registry, common data definitions, eligible calculations and other common infrastructure; they are not separate, disconnected products. - The Database Manager prepares, standardises, validates, registers and manages datasets. The Analytical Workbench selects and runs research against prepared datasets; it does not independently interpret arbitrary source files as part of its normal research workflow. - Original source files must not be modified by Q-MAP. Retention and deletion are separate policy choices; “untouched” means no alteration of source contents, not an automatic promise of indefinite retention. - Daily OHLCV is the baseline market-data layer. Minute-level data is initially focused on approximately 4–5 regularly used indices, and hourly data may be maintained for a selected universe of approximately 200–500 stocks. These are planning parameters, not fixed limits or evidence that data is already available. - Weekly, monthly, quarterly and yearly data may be derived from daily data under defined aggregation rules. Other derived timeframes should be generated as needed, with commonly used datasets eligible for retention where useful. - Permanent and Temporary are user-controlled storage classifications. Temporary data remains available until the user chooses to delete it; no automatic expiry is assumed initially. Moving or deleting managed datasets must update the registry and check known dependencies. Original source files are not to be overwritten. - Parquet remains the proposed durable market-data format, DuckDB the analytical query engine and SQLite the initial metadata/registry option, subject to technical validation. Data updates must handle duplicates and conflicts explicitly rather than blindly appending. - The next development step is an implementation audit and migration proposal, not an immediate rewrite. Existing working functionality should be preserved wherever possible. - Implementation status must be verified from code, tests and practical acceptance; this Blueprint describes intended behaviour, not proof of delivery.

1. Executive overview

Q-MAP stands for Quantitative Market Analytics Platform.

It is intended to become a personal, extensible historical market analytics and quantitative research environment. Its primary purpose is to investigate market behaviour systematically, test hypotheses against historical data, compare scenarios and combinations of conditions, and inspect the evidence behind the results.

Suitable research findings may later be reused to scan existing historical data for instruments meeting defined conditions. The initial platform does not require live data, broker connectivity or trade execution. Hypothetical historical entries, exits, targets, stops and costs may be simulated when needed for analytical evaluation.

Q-MAP brings together two existing bodies of work:

  • MIQP — Market Intelligence & Query Platform: the market-wide view of indices, market regime, breadth, sectors, relative strength and stock intelligence.
  • QLOPB — Quant Lens on Price Behaviour: the focused study of how price behaves around moving averages and other technical reference points.

They should become two major analytical capabilities within one platform, rather than two separate applications.

The platform should also support future research modules such as moving-average variants, RSI, MACD, stochastic and other oscillators, ATR and volatility, Bollinger Bands, traditional/Fibonacci/Camarilla pivots, CPR, candlestick behaviour, volume-price analysis, breakouts, pullbacks, reversals, consolidation, opening-range behaviour, relative strength and the owner’s own proprietary formulas and observations. This list is illustrative and extensible; no module needs to be activated or built until it is useful.

The four central questions

Question Q-MAP capability
What market behaviour repeats, and under what conditions? Historical research and scenario testing
Which conditions or combinations have useful evidence? Shared analytical components and research engine
Which instruments meet selected research rules at a historical date? Historical-data scanner
How would precisely defined hypothetical rules have behaved? Strategy backtesting and validation
How can I inspect and reproduce the evidence? Research Library, run history, charts and reports

What Q-MAP is — and is not

Q-MAP is a historical market analytics, quantitative research and decision-support platform. It is not initially intended to be a live-data service, automated trading bot, broker execution system or live position manager.

Its calculations and research results must work without AI. AI may help explain findings, help formulate research questions or assist with documentation, but it must not invent calculations or replace the research engine.

2. Why Q-MAP is being created

The original projects address related but different needs.

Existing project Main question it addresses Intended role in Q-MAP
MIQP What is happening across the market, sectors and stocks? Market Intelligence
QLOPB How has price behaved around a chosen technical reference? Price Behaviour Research

Keeping them as unrelated applications would lead to duplicated data handling, calculations, maintenance and research records. Combining every calculation into one tightly connected program would make it difficult to add or change research methods safely.

The preferred approach is therefore:

One platform, one shared foundation, independent analytical modules and a common way to store and reuse results.

The modules should be able to operate independently. They should also be able to use another module’s approved data or findings when a research question requires it.

3. What the platform should answer

Q-MAP should support the following types of questions.

Market questions

  • Is the overall market bullish, bearish, sideways or transitioning?
  • Is market breadth improving or deteriorating?
  • Which sectors are gaining or losing relative strength?
  • Is market leadership broadening or narrowing?
  • How does the current market compare with earlier periods?

Stock questions

  • Which stocks have strong price structure and relative performance?
  • Which stocks are improving or weakening?
  • Which stocks are leading their sectors?
  • What is the stock’s current relationship with important technical references?
  • What evidence supports its ranking or classification?

Price-behaviour questions

  • What tends to happen after price touches or crosses a moving average?
  • Does the behaviour change when the moving average is rising or falling?
  • Does a CPR breakout behave differently in a trending market?
  • How do candlestick patterns behave in different market regimes?
  • Does an Ichimoku Cloud interaction have a different outcome when other conditions agree?
  • Do the owner’s proprietary observations have measurable historical support?

Strategy questions

  • What exact entry and exit rules are being tested?
  • What would the historical trade record have looked like?
  • What are the returns, drawdowns, win rate and expectancy?
  • Does the result survive out-of-sample and walk-forward testing?
  • Does it remain useful after reasonable costs and execution assumptions?

4.3 Design principles

  1. One integrated Q-MAP platform with two major functional modules: Database and Analytics.
  2. Two separate user interfaces: Database Manager and Analytical Workbench.
  3. The Database Module is a prerequisite; each analysis must check readiness of its own required data.
  4. Independent research modules, not one monolithic research program.
  5. Common data definitions and calculation standards.
  6. Reuse existing data, features and findings wherever valid.
  7. Keep engines independent of their GUIs and keep shared services reusable.
  8. Keep source code, managed data and generated outputs physically separate.
  9. Support additional research methods and timeframes without redesigning the foundation.
  10. Make calculations and findings explainable and reproducible.
  11. Build only the complexity justified by actual needs; audit before rewriting.

4.4 Two major modules and two separate GUIs

Q-MAP has two major functional modules:

  1. Database Module — imports source data, interprets it using explicit source configurations, standardises and validates it, manages datasets and records their status in the Data Registry.
  2. Analytical Module — runs MIQP, QLOPB and future quantitative research against suitable standardised datasets, and manages research outputs and reproducibility.

The Database Module is a prerequisite for the Analytical Module. This does not mean every dataset must be complete before any analysis can run. The Analytical Workbench checks the specific instruments, date range, timeframe, columns and quality requirements needed by each requested analysis. It may allow an analysis when its required data is ready, even if unrelated datasets have not been prepared. It should explain missing or unsuitable data and must not silently bypass relevant quality controls.

The two user interfaces are:

  • Database Manager GUI — used to prepare, validate, register, inspect and manage market data.
  • Analytical Workbench GUI — used to select research modules, define parameters, run analysis and inspect results.

The GUIs are separate, but they are not separate products. They share the Data Registry, canonical data definitions, common calculation services where appropriate, configuration conventions and provenance standards.

Target application entry points

The current target is one Q-MAP_APP folder with two main Python entry-point files:

  • database_manager.py — launches the Database Manager GUI.
  • analytical_workbench.py — launches the Analytical Workbench GUI.

Shared code and specialised database/analytical components sit alongside these entry points. This is a target design, not a claim about the current repository. The exact internal directory structure and module boundaries must be confirmed by inspecting the existing code before implementation. Optionally separate packaging or deployment arrangements may be considered later if justified; they are not required for the initial design.

The entry-point files launch interfaces; they should not contain the full engines. Database processing and quantitative calculations belong in reusable engine components so that GUI changes do not require rewriting core logic and future automation remains possible.

Data-readiness handshake

Before starting a run, the Analytical Workbench should verify, using the Data Registry and the underlying files or query interface, that:

  • the required dataset is registered and its location is known;
  • required files are present and readable;
  • instrument, date coverage, timeframe and required columns match the analysis;
  • data-quality status and known limitations are visible and compatible with the analysis;
  • the requested calculation does not silently rely on missing, stale or incompatible data.

The registry alone is not proof that a file still exists or is readable. Conversely, files found on disk should not be treated as approved datasets without registration and appropriate validation. The database workflow should publish a clear readiness status and relevant metadata for the Analytical Workbench to consume.

Development rule

Before changing the existing GUI or reorganising the code, the developer must inspect the current implementation, map existing functions to the two modules, identify reusable components and propose a migration plan. No full rewrite should be assumed. Preserve working functionality where it fits the target architecture, and use feature branches, pull requests, tests and Project Owner approval before merging into main.

5. Application windows and how they are used

The application should present a coherent set of windows. These are functional areas, not necessarily separate programs.

5.1 Dashboard — daily starting point

Purpose: Give a concise view of the market and highlight where attention may be useful.

Expected content:

  • Major indices and their current market condition.
  • Market regime and breadth.
  • Strongest and weakest sectors.
  • Volatility context.
  • Stocks meeting saved screening criteria.
  • Recent research results and important data updates.

Typical use: Open Q-MAP, understand the broad environment and decide which areas deserve further investigation.

5.2 Market Overview

Purpose: Examine the market as a whole.

Expected content:

  • Index trend and moving-average structure.
  • Market breadth, including the percentage of stocks above selected moving averages.
  • Advance/decline information and new highs/lows, where data is available.
  • Market volatility.
  • Market-regime classification.
  • Historical market conditions for a selected date.

Typical use: Establish whether the broader environment is supportive, mixed or unfavourable for a particular type of trading.

5.3 Sector Explorer

Purpose: Identify where relative strength and weakness are developing.

Expected content:

  • Sector performance over different periods.
  • Relative strength against the market.
  • Sector trend and breadth.
  • Sector rotation and historical comparisons.
  • Constituent stocks and their contribution to sector performance.

Typical use: Narrow the market to sectors that deserve closer stock-level research.

5.4 Stock Explorer

Purpose: Provide a detailed profile of one instrument.

Expected content:

  • Daily, weekly and other available timeframe charts.
  • Price, volume and selected technical references.
  • Trend and stage classification.
  • Relative performance against the market and sector.
  • Relevant saved price-behaviour findings.
  • Evidence supporting classifications and rankings.

Typical use: Move from a market or sector observation to a detailed assessment of an individual stock.

5.5 Opportunity Scanner

Purpose: Combine selected market, sector, stock and price-behaviour conditions to identify candidates.

Example conditions:

Condition Potential source
Market regime is bullish Market Intelligence
Sector is outperforming Sector Intelligence
Stock has a favourable trend classification Market Intelligence
Price is above a rising moving average Common calculations
Historical pullbacks to that reference have favourable evidence Price Behaviour Research
Volume confirms the condition Volume Research

The user should be able to save screening definitions, adjust conditions and see why each stock qualified. The initial scanner operates on existing historical data and can evaluate a chosen historical date or data cut-off. Daily, weekly and monthly rules should use completed bars when that is what the rule requires; an unfinished period must not be treated as completed.

Rules and calculations should be reusable from research rather than independently reimplemented in the scanner. The scanner may show that an instrument meets a research rule, but should not imply a guaranteed future outcome. If a research rule has limited or mixed evidence, that limitation should remain visible.

A single composite score should not hide the underlying evidence. Individual conditions and their contributions must remain visible.

5.6 Price Behaviour Lab

Purpose: Investigate historical price behaviour and test research hypotheses.

Potential workbenches:

  • Moving-average interactions.
  • CPR and pivot behaviour.
  • Ichimoku Cloud behaviour.
  • Candlestick patterns and combinations.
  • Volume-price behaviour.
  • Breakouts, pullbacks, gaps and other price events.
  • Regime-conditioned and sector-conditioned research.
  • Proprietary observations.

A typical experiment should define the universe, timeframe, historical period, event or condition, parameters, forward-return windows and relevant evaluation approach. Results should include sample size and evidence, not only a ranking.

The workbench should support both module-specific research and a flexible general-purpose workflow that lets the owner combine compatible analytical components. Examples include all-time-high breakouts with trend, relative-strength, volume, volatility, market-regime or sector conditions; pivot/CPR behaviour with candlestick patterns; or custom indicators combined with standard features.

The system should make it practical to compare a baseline with additional conditions, parameter values and combinations. It should not assume that a more complex combination is better. Where practical, the user should be able to drill from summary statistics into individual historical cases and charts.

5.7 Strategy Backtesting Lab

Purpose: Test complete trading rules rather than only describe historical price behaviour.

A strategy definition may include:

  • Eligible universe.
  • Market and sector filters.
  • Entry conditions.
  • Entry execution assumption.
  • Stop-loss and target.
  • Exit rules.
  • Position sizing.
  • Brokerage, taxes, slippage and other relevant costs.
  • Portfolio-level constraints.

Expected outputs include an equity curve, trade list, returns, CAGR, drawdown, win rate, expectancy, risk-adjusted measures and robustness results.

A completed price-behaviour study is not automatically a profitable trading strategy. The strategy must be separately defined and tested.

5.8 Portfolio and Risk

Purpose: Understand the combined risk of positions and strategies.

Potential capabilities:

  • Position sizing.
  • Risk per trade.
  • Sector and instrument concentration.
  • Correlation between positions.
  • Portfolio exposure.
  • Drawdown and risk limits.
  • Strategy-level and portfolio-level performance.

This can begin with simulated portfolios. Live portfolio and broker connections are not required for the initial platform.

5.9 Research Library

Purpose: Preserve the work already completed so that it can be found and reused.

Each saved experiment or finding should record:

  • Research ID and title.
  • Originating module.
  • Research question.
  • Universe and timeframe.
  • Data period and dataset version.
  • Parameters and methodology version.
  • Sample size and results.
  • In-sample and out-of-sample evidence.
  • Status and limitations.
  • Related experiments or strategies.

Suggested lightweight, owner-controlled statuses:

Status Meaning
Under Research An idea is being investigated; this can include initial exploratory tests
Promising Initial results look interesting, but the owner may want more evidence
Validated The owner considers the evidence sufficient for the intended use
Archived No longer actively used, but retained for history and reproducibility

Statuses are labels, not mandatory workflow gates. The owner may change a status directly, combine the practical meaning of statuses, return an idea to research, or mark an idea validated based on work performed elsewhere. Q-MAP may display useful cautions about sample size, robustness or validation, but should not impose unnecessary administrative steps.

The status records the owner’s assessment; it does not guarantee that a pattern will persist or that a strategy will be profitable. Technical calculation validation, evidence about a research hypothesis and permission to use a rule in scanning are distinct concepts and should not be conflated.

5.10 Database Manager (Database GUI)

Purpose: Prepare and manage the data foundation used by all Q-MAP research. This is a separate GUI and the first application to use when new or updated market data must be prepared.

Expected capabilities:

  • Register and inspect source files and explicit source profiles.
  • Import supported file formats, including CSV, Excel and Parquet where implemented.
  • Standardise source data into Q-MAP’s canonical market-data structure.
  • Identify and report duplicates, missing records, timestamp problems and OHLC/volume quality issues.
  • Maintain instrument, timeframe, source and relevant corporate-action metadata.
  • View data coverage and readiness by instrument, date range and timeframe.
  • Manage Permanent and Temporary datasets and report storage/processing status.
  • Preserve the original source file contents; never overwrite or silently alter an original.
  • Publish registry records and readiness information for the Analytical Workbench.

Data preparation should be explicit and auditable. The application must not silently guess ambiguous date formats, volume meanings, timezones or other material source semantics. It should report unresolved issues and retain provenance for transformations.

5.11 Analytical Workbench (Analytical GUI)

Purpose: Provide the separate interface for research and analysis using prepared, registered datasets.

Expected capabilities:

  • Select MIQP, QLOPB or another available research module.
  • Select instruments/universes, date ranges, timeframes and analytical parameters.
  • Check required dataset readiness before execution.
  • Run supported calculations and research without duplicating source-file interpretation logic.
  • Display findings, limitations, charts and reports.
  • Store each run’s parameters, dataset references, code/method version where available, Run ID and outputs.
  • Explain which data is missing or unsuitable when a requested analysis cannot proceed.

The Analytical Workbench should not be responsible for general-purpose source-file ingestion and standardisation. If data is missing, it should explain the requirement and direct the user to the Database Manager rather than silently creating an alternate data-preparation path.

New research ideas must be addable without rebuilding the platform.

6.2 What every module should provide

Each module should have a consistent description and operating standard:

  1. What question does it investigate?
  2. What input data does it require?
  3. Which timeframes does it support?
  4. Which parameters can the user change?
  5. What calculations and events does it produce?
  6. What research outputs does it generate?
  7. How is its work validated?
  8. Which other modules may use its outputs?
  9. Which version of the module produced the result?

A module must be independently testable. It should not need to know the internal code of another module.

Modules should expose clearly defined inputs, parameters, outputs, timeframe assumptions and version information. Compatible outputs should be usable by the common research engine and scanner. New ideas should be added through a module or configuration using the common framework—not by creating a separate bespoke application for every idea. Modules that are not selected for use can remain dormant and should not consume significant resources merely by existing.

Research must distinguish the chart/bar timeframe from the calculation timeframe. For example, a weekly pivot or CPR value may be examined against hourly bars, but only using the values that would have been available at each historical point. Aggregation, exchange sessions, holidays, shortened sessions, missing bars and timezones must be handled consistently.

6.3 Example: Adding Ichimoku research

A future Ichimoku module could:

  1. Calculate the standard Ichimoku components.
  2. Identify price above, below or within the Cloud.
  3. Identify Cloud breakouts and interactions.
  4. Record relationships between price and the relevant components.
  5. Study forward returns after defined events.
  6. Compare behaviour under different market regimes and sectors.
  7. validate findings on data not used to select the hypothesis.
  8. Publish its results to the Research Library.

The same approach applies to a proprietary observation. The observation must first be translated into clear, testable rules. It can then use the common data, research and validation services.

7. How modules share and reuse work

This is a central feature of Q-MAP.

Independent does not mean isolated. Modules should be able to use standardised data and outputs from other modules without depending on their internal implementation.

7.1 Example of combined research

Research question:

What happens when a stock is in Stage 2, above a rising 50-day moving average, above the Ichimoku Cloud and forms a bullish engulfing candle near a selected reference?

Information required Source
Stage classification Market Intelligence
50-day moving average and slope Common Calculation Library
Ichimoku Cloud position Ichimoku Research
Bullish engulfing event Candlestick Research
Subsequent returns Common Research Framework
Market-regime condition Market Intelligence

The common research framework combines the conditions and evaluates the resulting historical observations.

7.2 Common calculation library

Standard calculations should be implemented once and reused wherever appropriate.

Examples include:

  • Moving averages.
  • ATR and volatility.
  • CPR and pivot calculations.
  • Volume averages.
  • Relative strength.
  • Standard price and return calculations.
  • Timeframe aggregation.

The definition of a calculation must be consistent. If a methodology changes, the new version must be identified rather than silently changing historical results.

7.3 Common Feature Store

Q-MAP should retain commonly used calculated values in a reusable analytical store.

For example, the 50-day moving average for an instrument and date should not be recalculated independently by MIQP, QLOPB and every new research module.

The Feature Store should record enough information to identify:

  • Instrument.
  • Date and timeframe.
  • Feature name and value.
  • Calculation version.
  • Source data version.
  • Relevant parameters.

Modules should retrieve the feature when it is valid and current. They should request recalculation when the data or methodology requires it.

7.4 Research findings are reusable too

The Research Library stores conclusions and evidence, not just raw calculated values.

For example, a completed study of 30-week moving-average support behaviour can be used by the Opportunity Scanner or a later strategy experiment without rerunning the original research.

The receiving module must still check that the finding applies to its requested universe, timeframe, period and methodology.

8.3 Handling large data volumes

Intraday data can become much larger than daily data. Q-MAP should therefore avoid loading entire historical datasets into memory or placing every raw market record in one database file.

Proposed storage responsibilities:

Storage component Proposed role
Parquet files Durable storage for standardised historical market data and retained reusable datasets; organised by sensible dimensions/partitions rather than one database per stock
DuckDB Analytical query engine over Parquet and other supported tabular data
SQLite initially Application metadata, Data Registry, dataset status, settings and operational records, subject to technical validation
PostgreSQL later, if justified Larger multi-user or concurrent operational needs; not an initial requirement

Permanent and Temporary are managed-data classifications, not different analytical formats. The proposed Temporary location is Q-MAP_DATA/Temporary/; the final directory layout should be confirmed during implementation planning. Temporary datasets do not automatically expire initially. The user chooses when to delete them.

The user should be able to change a managed dataset’s classification between Permanent and Temporary after an analysis has completed. Such a move must update the Data Registry and any stored path references. Before deletion or a move that may break access, Q-MAP should check known dependencies and warn clearly. The application should not silently break a saved research run or reusable feature’s data lineage. Moving data should not be confused with modifying its contents.

Permanent datasets should include the standardised historical market data selected for ongoing use. The system should not automatically retain every intermediate calculation or every possible timeframe. Reusable features—such as moving averages, pivots, volume measures, bands and average daily range—may be retained when useful, with their definitions, parameters, source dataset/version and calculation provenance recorded.

Updates must not blindly append new rows. Ingestion/update logic should detect duplicates, overlaps and conflicts, apply an explicit approved resolution policy, preserve provenance and report what was added, changed, rejected or left unresolved. Raw originals must not be overwritten. SQLite and the precise Parquet/DuckDB organisation remain subject to a technical audit and validation against actual access patterns.

8.4 Data quality and time alignment

The platform should check:

  • Missing and duplicate records.
  • Invalid or inconsistent OHLC values.
  • Date and timestamp formats.
  • Exchange sessions and trading calendars.
  • Timezone handling.
  • Corporate actions and price adjustments.
  • Instrument changes and symbol mapping.
  • Data coverage and gaps.

Higher-timeframe bars derived from intraday data must follow consistent exchange-session and aggregation rules.

10. Research workflow and backtesting

Q-MAP must distinguish between discovering a historical relationship, scanning historical data for qualifying instruments, and testing a complete hypothetical trading strategy.

10.1 Three connected capabilities

Historical Research Lab

The Research Lab investigates hypotheses using historical OHLCV and other registered datasets. It should support single conditions, combinations, parameter sweeps, multiple instruments, historical periods, market regimes and compatible timeframes. The owner should be able to compare results, inspect historical examples and save the experiment.

Historical-data Scanner

The scanner applies selected, clearly defined rules to existing historical data at a chosen historical date or data cut-off. It should reuse the same calculations and rule definitions used by research wherever compatible. It identifies instruments that meet the criteria; it does not promise future performance.

Strategy Backtesting

A backtest simulates a complete set of hypothetical historical rules, including entry timing, exits, stops/targets, holding periods and relevant costs where applicable. It is a separate analysis from measuring a price pattern or scanning for conditions. It does not require live execution.

The initial scope is historical data only. A future live-data source may be considered separately, but the data-access design should avoid unnecessarily coupling calculations to one data source. Live feeds, broker connectivity, order routing and live position management are not part of the initial scope.

10.2 Research workflow

The workflow is deliberately flexible rather than a mandatory sequence. A user may record an idea, run exploratory tests, review results, adjust parameters, mark a finding as promising or validated, and choose whether to use it for scanning. Q-MAP should automate run records, provenance and routine comparisons without requiring the user to administer a complex process.

10.3 Example research flow

flowchart TB
    A["Research question or hypothesis"]
    B["Select data, universe and timeframe"]
    C["Define event, conditions and parameters"]
    D["Run historical analysis"]
    E["Review sample size and evidence"]
    F["In-sample selection and out-of-sample validation"]
    G["Save finding and methodology"]
    H["Use finding in screening or strategy research"]

    A --> B --> C --> D --> E --> F --> G --> H

10.4 Price-behaviour research

Example: study how price behaves when it interacts with a rising 30-week moving average.

The study could measure:

  • Number of qualifying interactions.
  • Positive and negative outcomes.
  • Average and median forward returns.
  • Return distribution.
  • Favourable and adverse price movement, where defined.
  • Differences across market regimes.
  • In-sample and out-of-sample consistency.

The output describes historical behaviour. It does not by itself prove that a profitable trading strategy exists.

10.5 Strategy backtesting

A complete strategy test must define the actual trading rules.

Example:

Rule Illustrative definition
Universe Selected stock universe
Market filter Bullish regime
Sector filter Sector outperforming
Stock filter Defined trend condition
Entry Defined pullback and confirmation
Execution Next eligible trading session
Stop-loss Defined ATR-based rule
Target Defined reward-to-risk rule
Position sizing Fixed portfolio risk per trade
Exit Stop, target or time-based rule

These are example rules, not a validated trading system.

10.6 Validation requirements

Backtesting should address:

  • Chronological in-sample and out-of-sample separation.
  • Walk-forward testing.
  • Look-ahead bias.
  • Survivorship bias and historical constituent coverage.
  • Corporate actions and adjusted prices.
  • Brokerage, taxes, slippage and execution assumptions.
  • Overlapping signals and portfolio constraints.
  • Sensitivity to parameter changes.
  • Detailed trade-level records.

A strategy should not be labelled validated simply because its historical return is attractive.

11. Front-end choices

The database and analytical engines should be independent of their respective user interfaces. Q-MAP will expose two separate GUIs: Database Manager for data preparation/management, and Analytical Workbench for research/analysis.

Python should perform the calculations, data transformations, validation and backtesting. Each GUI should request work, display results and provide user controls. The two GUIs share the same platform services and data registry; neither should duplicate core calculations or maintain a disconnected copy of dataset metadata.

13. Research integrity and quality controls

The platform’s usefulness depends on the quality of its data, calculations and methodology.

The following rules should apply across all modules.

  1. Deterministic calculations: The same inputs, parameters and code version should produce the same results.
  2. Independent verification: Important calculations should be independently checkable, not hidden behind a dashboard.
  3. Visible evidence: Scores and rankings should show their underlying factors and supporting observations.
  4. No look-ahead: Historical calculations must use only information available at the time being tested.
  5. No silent data changes: Original input files remain preserved; cleaned data is separately identifiable.
  6. Versioned methodology: Changes to formulas or event definitions must be recorded.
  7. Appropriate validation: Research selection and final validation must use separate historical observations.
  8. Honest status labels: Insufficient evidence must not be presented as a negative result or as proof of failure.
  9. Costs and execution: Strategy tests must state their execution and cost assumptions.
  10. AI independence: The platform’s core calculations must work without AI.
  11. Scenario-comparison discipline: Report sample sizes and useful baselines; do not assume additional filters improve a result.
  12. Multiple-testing caution: When many parameter combinations are tried, make the exploratory nature visible and encourage separate-period or out-of-sample checks where practical.
  13. Historical scanner integrity: A scan for a past date may use only data available at that date. Future data must not affect historical qualification.
  14. User-controlled workflow: Status labels and research process should assist rather than constrain the owner.
  15. Evidence over labels: A status such as Validated records the owner’s judgment; supporting evidence and limitations remain accessible.

13.1 Reusing work without repeating it

Q-MAP should include a Research Run Manager and a result-reuse mechanism.

When an experiment is requested, the system should check whether an equivalent result already exists.

It should compare:

  • Dataset and data version.
  • Instrument universe.
  • Timeframe and date range.
  • Module and code version.
  • Calculation methodology.
  • Research parameters.

If all relevant inputs match, an existing result may be reused.

If data, methodology or parameters have changed, the system should create a new run or recalculate the affected information.

Incremental processing should be used where practical. For example, when new daily data arrives, the system should calculate the new affected periods rather than unnecessarily rebuilding every historical feature.

A reused result must retain its original Run ID and provenance. The system must not make an old result appear to have been generated using newer data or code.

16. Project governance and working principles

These rules should guide future discussions, development and changes.

16.1 Owner approval

The platform owner approves material decisions involving scope, research methodology, architecture, data policy and front-end selection.

16.2 Documentation before material changes

A material change should be described in the Master Blueprint or its supporting specifications before implementation. Small implementation details do not need to interrupt the main document.

For the two-GUI architecture, the first technical action is an audit and proposal: inspect the existing application, map functions to Database Manager and Analytical Workbench, identify reusable components, describe the required data-readiness contract and propose a migration sequence. The proposal should state what can be retained, what must change, test/acceptance criteria, risks and dependencies. Do not start a broad refactor or rewrite until the proposal has been reviewed and the Project Owner has approved the material direction.

16.3 Change history

Each material decision should record:

  • Decision reference and date.
  • What was previously proposed.
  • What has changed.
  • Why it changed.
  • Which modules or documents are affected.
  • Approval status.

16.4 Development and testing

A new module should include its own tests and documentation. Changes to shared calculations should trigger regression testing of the modules that use them.

16.5 No unnecessary rewrites

Working, tested code should be reused where it fits the agreed architecture. The decision to separate the GUIs does not by itself imply that the engines must be rewritten. Rewriting should have a clear benefit and a controlled migration plan supported by an inspection of the existing implementation.

16.6 No hidden dependencies

A research module should declare what data, features and services it needs. It should not rely on another module’s private internal code or silently modify its outputs.

Closing statement

Q-MAP should be built as a long-term historical market analytics and quantitative research environment, not as a fixed collection of indicators or a one-purpose trading scanner.

Its value should increase as more historical data is registered, more research modules are added, more findings are evaluated and more of the previous work becomes reusable. The system should be powerful behind the scenes but straightforward to operate: automate repetitive work, show evidence clearly and leave research priorities and judgments with the owner.

The guiding idea is simple:

Prepare and register market data through the Database Manager. Analyse suitable data through the Analytical Workbench. Maintain two separate GUIs over one shared platform. Build the foundation once, reuse valid calculations and findings, preserve research provenance, and change existing code carefully rather than rewriting it without evidence.

This document is the consolidated starting point for that work. It should evolve through controlled, recorded changes as Q-MAP moves from design into implementation.
